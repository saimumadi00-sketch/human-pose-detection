01 · Fundamentals

What is Pose Estimation?

Human pose estimation is the computer vision task of localising anatomical keypoints (joints) in images or video — essentially teaching a neural network to understand where limbs, torso, and head are in pixel-space.

The output is typically a set of (x, y, confidence) tuples — one per keypoint. Common skeletons use 17 joints (COCO format) up to 135 points for whole-body models. The main challenge: heavy occlusion, varied lighting, clothing, and scale.

👤

Single-Person Estimation

One person in the frame. Simpler pipeline — the network directly regresses or detects keypoints without resolving identity. Faster and often more accurate per-person.

👥

Multi-Person Estimation

Multiple overlapping people. Requires either detecting people first (top-down) or grouping detected keypoints into individuals (bottom-up). Much harder to solve correctly.

📐

2D vs 3D Pose

2D pose predicts image-plane (x,y) coordinates. 3D pose lifts those to world-space depth, requiring either stereo cameras, depth sensors, or learned monocular depth priors.

🎞️

Video-Based Pose

Temporal models (RNNs, transformers with time dimension) exploit motion continuity to smooth jitter and recover occluded joints invisible in a single frame.

📍

Output

Heatmap vs. Regression: Most SOTA models predict a Gaussian heatmap per keypoint — the peak gives the location. Direct coordinate regression is simpler but harder to train to the same accuracy. Heatmaps are spatially smoother and easier for the loss function to optimise.

03 · Architectures

Key Model Architectures

The field evolved rapidly — from stacked hourglasses to lightweight mobile models to transformer-based approaches.

01

Stacked Hourglass (Newell et al., 2016)

CNN Single/Multi Heatmap

The landmark architecture. Multiple encoder–decoder "hourglass" modules stacked in series, each taking the previous output as input. Intermediate supervision at each stack trains the network to repeatedly refine its estimates. Still a baseline benchmark today.

02

SimpleBaseline / HRNet (Sun et al., 2019)

CNN Top-Down High-Resolution

HRNet maintains high-resolution representations throughout the entire network — no downsampling to a bottleneck — by running multiple branches at different scales in parallel and repeatedly fusing them. Achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on COCO and MPII with excellent spatial precision.

03

OpenPose (Cao et al., 2017)

CNN Bottom-Up Part Affinity Fields

Introduced Part Affinity Fields (PAFs) — vector fields encoding the orientation of limbs between joints. All keypoints detected first, then PAFs used to associate them into individual skeletons. Enables real-time multi-person inference since detection scales independently of person count.

04

MediaPipe BlazePose

Lightweight CNN Single-Person Mobile / Real-Time

Google's on-device model optimised for mobile inference. Uses a two-stage pipeline: a fast detector to crop the person ROI, then a lightweight regressor for 33 3D keypoints at 30+ fps on CPU. Powers Google Meet, Pixel camera effects, and many fitness apps.

05

ViTPose (Xu et al., 2022)

Vision Transformer Top-Down SOTA

Uses a plain Vision Transformer (ViT) as the backbone — no special multi-resolution design needed. Massive pre-training (MAE on ImageNet) gives rich representations. A simple decoder head with deconvolutions produces the keypoint heatmaps. Sets COCO records and demonstrates transformers can supersede CNNs for pose.

06

YOLO-Pose / RTMPose

CNN + Anchor-Free One-Stage Real-Time

Single-shot detectors extended to jointly predict bounding boxes and keypoints in one pass. RTMPose from OpenMMLab achieves an excellent speed-accuracy trade-off for production deployments, running at 90+ fps on GPU. Ideal for edge and latency-sensitive applications.

04 · Comparison

Quick Reference

Choosing the right model depends on speed requirements, hardware constraints, and whether you need single or multi-person support.

Model Approach Speed Accuracy Best For
Stacked Hourglass Top-down / CNN Moderate Good Research baselines
HRNet Top-down / CNN Moderate Excellent Accuracy-critical tasks
OpenPose Bottom-up / CNN Fast (multi) Good Crowd scenes
BlazePose Top-down / Lightweight Very Fast Good Mobile / on-device
ViTPose Top-down / ViT Slow SOTA Offline, max accuracy
RTMPose One-stage / CNN Very Fast Very Good Production / real-time
05 · Applications

Where It's Used

Pose estimation has become a foundational primitive in a surprisingly wide range of domains.

🏋️

Fitness & Rehab

Rep counting, form correction, physical therapy exercises — real-time feedback without wearables.

🎮

AR / Gaming

Body-driven avatars, gesture controls, full-body AR effects in video calls and mobile games.

🏥

Healthcare

Fall detection in elderly care, surgical assistance, gait analysis, and pain assessment.

🎭

Animation / VFX

Markerless motion capture for film, game character rigging, and real-time performance retargeting.

🏟️

Sports Analytics

Biomechanics analysis, performance metrics, broadcast enhancements, coaching insights.

🛡️

Surveillance

Anomaly detection (falls, fights, loitering), activity recognition in security and retail.

06 · Training

Training Deep Pose Models

Datasets: COCO (200k images, 17 keypoints), MPII (25k images, 16 keypoints), and Human3.6M (3.6M frames, 17 joints, 3D) are the standard benchmarks. Whole-body variants (COCO-WholeBody) add 133 keypoints including hands and face.

Loss function: Most models optimise an MSE loss on Gaussian heatmaps. Some use OKS (Object Keypoint Similarity) — the official COCO metric — directly as a differentiable loss. For regression heads, smooth L1 or wing loss handles outliers better than MSE.

Data augmentation: Random crop, scale, rotation, horizontal flip, and — critically — half-body augmentation (randomly crop to upper or lower body) forces models to learn from partial observations and dramatically improves occlusion robustness.

Pre-training matters enormously. ImageNet classification pre-training on the backbone gives a large head start. MAE (Masked Autoencoder) self-supervised pre-training, as used in ViTPose, delivers further gains without labelled data.
